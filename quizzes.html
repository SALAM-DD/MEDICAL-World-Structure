A 58-year-old male with a 20-year history of hypertension presents to the emergency department with acute retrosternal chest pressure radiating to his left jaw. ECG reveals ST-segment elevation in leads II, III, and aVF. Which of the following coronary arteries is most likely occluded?
✓ Correct Answer: B) Right Coronary Artery (RCA)

High-Yield Explanation: Leads II, III, and aVF correspond to the inferior wall of the left ventricle. In approximately 85-90% of individuals, the inferior wall is supplied by the posterior descending artery arising from the Right Coronary Artery (RCA dominance).
